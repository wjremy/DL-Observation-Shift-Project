I read `docs/GAP.md`. The architecture literature points to one gap relevant to your proposal: most irregular-series models fuse measurement patterns with physiology in one representation. A few model observation timing or missingness separately, but they do not test the proposed small, labeled, site-local adaptation of a testing component.

### Models of the observation process

The clearest explicit value/process split is [Alaa, Hu, and van der Schaar (2017)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/alaa17a.html): a latent-state-modulated marked Hawkes process models clinicians’ measurement events, while a switching multitask Gaussian process models physiological values. The two components are coupled through latent clinical states; the paper does not test adapting the event model alone at a new hospital. [Lasko (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v29i1.9456) likewise estimates physiology and volatility separately from a sampling-rate function inferred from times alone, but is a statistical sampling study rather than a deep risk predictor.

[Neural TPPs](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v136/enguehard20a.html) and [POPCORN](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v149/bhave21a.html) model EHR events and their timing as a generative process; their event stream does not provide a distinct, adaptable physiology head and testing head. [DeepJoint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.13481), already in GAP, is the closer neural precedent: it has parallel outputs for longitudinal observations, inter-observation times, missingness, and survival, from a shared recurrent encoder. It demonstrates separate heads, but not independent local adaptation of the testing heads.

### Observation-process shift specifically

I did **not find** a controlled clinical study comparing GRU-D, Latent ODE, Neural CDE, mTAN, SeFT, Raindrop, or their successors under an intentionally changed measurement schedule. The closest deep-model test is [Singh, Sato, and Ohkuma (2021)](https://doi.org/10.2196/25022): a GRU with binary missingness features was evaluated across PhysioNet hospital systems. Missingness features helped retrospective tasks, but in simulated prospective sepsis prediction the physiology-only model beat the indicator-augmented model by 0.9% AUROC; this was one architecture and did not perturb sampling rates directly. The [Yamamoto et al. 2026 preprint](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.04.05.26350209), already noted in GAP, is a closer cross-dataset ablation of measurement counts, but uses logistic regression and boosted trees rather than the irregular-series architectures above. Tranchellini et al. also report that direct missingness-shift analysis remains future work.

For a two-page proposal, I would cite six works: **Che et al. (2018)** for explicit masks and gaps; **Rubanova et al. (2019)** for continuous-time dynamics and an optional observation-time likelihood; **Alaa et al. (2017)** for separate physiological and measurement-process models; **Jeanselme et al. (2022)** for neural observation-process heads; **Moor et al. (2023)** for multicenter deep sepsis transfer; and **Yamamoto et al. (2026)** for recent direct evidence about observation-feature transportability, clearly labeled as a preprint. Together they motivate both the architecture and the gap your adaptation ladder addresses.
