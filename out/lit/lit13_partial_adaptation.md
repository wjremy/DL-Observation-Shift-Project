# Literature report: adapting part of a model with few target labels

## 1. Partial adaptation methods

| Method | Part and parameters adapted | Target labels and result |
|---|---|---|
| Surgical fine-tuning, 2023, ICLR — [Lee et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2210.11466) | Tune one contiguous block of layers; parameter count depends on architecture and chosen block. | Experiments used 1,000 labeled CIFAR-10-C images, 5,000 ImageNet-C images, or 50 images per class on two subpopulation-shift datasets. The best block depended on shift: early layers for image corruptions, later layers for feature shifts, and the last layer for output shifts. A selected block could outperform full fine-tuning on small target sets. |
| Deep Feature Reweighting (DFR), 2023, ICLR — [Kirichenko et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2204.02937) | Freeze the feature extractor; retrain the last logistic-regression head. For binary Waterbirds with 2,048-dimensional features, that is about 2,049 coefficients. | About 500 balanced reweighting examples on Waterbirds improved worst-group accuracy from 74.9% to 92.9%. This is evidence for head-only adaptation when the representation already contains useful features, not a hospital-transfer threshold. |
| Linear probe, then fine-tune (LP-FT), 2022, ICLR — [Kumar et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2202.10054) | First fit a linear head (roughly *d × K* weights plus bias); then fine-tune the full network from that initialization. | Across 10 distribution-shift datasets, full fine-tuning averaged 2 points better in-distribution but 7 points worse OOD than probing; LP-FT beat full fine-tuning by 1 point ID and 10 OOD. The paper gives no universal target-label threshold. |
| Adapters, LoRA, BitFit — [Houlsby et al.](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/houlsby19a.html), [Hu et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09685), [Ben Zaken et al.](https://aclanthology.org/2022.acl-short.1/) | Adapters add 3.6% task-specific parameters to BERT; LoRA freezes base weights and adds low-rank matrices (10,000× fewer trainable parameters in the GPT-3 175B example); BitFit tunes biases, or a subset amounting to 0.04% of BERT parameters. | Adapters came within 0.4 percentage points of full fine-tuning on GLUE; BitFit was competitive on small-to-medium NLP training sets. These papers establish parameter-efficiency options, not how many clinical labels each needs. |
| Time-series domain/test-time adaptation — [RAINCOAT](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/he23b.html), [TAFAS](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/33965), [PETSA](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.23424) | RAINCOAT aligns temporal and frequency features without target labels. TAFAS freezes the forecaster and updates gated calibration modules using partially observed ground truth; PETSA updates low-rank input/output calibration modules. | RAINCOAT reported gains up to 16.33% across five datasets. PETSA reported comparable or better forecasting error with up to 33.6× fewer adaptation parameters than TAFAS in one setting. Here the adaptation signal is observed time-series values, not a small number of clinician-labeled outcome cases. |

These studies support a **capacity ladder**, but do not supply a general mapping from target-label count to best layer. In particular, a smaller parameter set is not itself evidence that a method will work with only a few outcome events.

## 2. Clinical transfer studies

| Study and setting | Model and target data | Reported benefit / sample threshold |
|---|---|---|
| [Desautels et al. (2017)](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1178222617712994), BIDMC → UCSF | AutoTriage mortality model; 48,249 target inpatients. Statistical transfer, not neural partial fine-tuning. | Transfer reduced target training data needed to exceed AUROC 0.80 from over 4,000 patients to fewer than 220. This is patient count, not a reported event-count threshold. |
| [Gupta et al. (2018)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.01705), MIMIC-III tasks | Pretrained multitask RNN features with a target linear classifier for new phenotypes and in-hospital mortality; within-database task transfer, not hospital transfer. | More robust to shrinking target datasets than task-specific RNNs; no single break-even sample count was reported. |
| [Chen et al. (2023)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12911-022-02090-3), MIMIC-III → Ruijin | LightGBM and MLP; cleaned target cohort of 453 patients, including 144 sepsis-positive patients. For the MLP, the first three of six layers were initially frozen, then the model was unfrozen for further tuning. | Transfer ensemble AUC was about 0.94 versus 0.82–0.87 for MIMIC-only models; target-only models reached about 0.92. The paper varied target sampling ratios but does not give a clear minimum target sample at which fine-tuning begins to help. |
| [van de Water et al. (2024)](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/c26a89073d972f2e6643617b0f3a9e8a-Paper-Conference.pdf), eICU → HiRID | GRU for ICU mortality; target-data sweep up to 10,000 HiRID samples. | Fine-tuning beat training from scratch at every tested sample size, with the largest advantage below 4,000 samples. |
| [Guo et al. (2024)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-024-01166-w), Stanford FM → SickKids and MIMIC-IV | Shared structured-EHR foundation model, evaluated on eight clinical tasks; local continued pretraining followed by task adaptation. | With continued local pretraining, 128 labeled examples (64 per class; under 1% of local data) matched a GBM trained on all available data. Off-the-shelf adaptation improved performance by 13% in few-task-label settings. |

**None of these studies tests adapting only a testing/observation subnetwork with 2–6 positive sepsis cases.** The closest direct sepsis-transfer evidence above used a target cohort with 144 positive cases.

## Updating ladder and events per parameter

Clinical prediction-model updating commonly proceeds from the original model to intercept-only recalibration (one parameter), logistic recalibration (intercept and slope), revision of some or all existing coefficients, and redevelopment/refitting. Vergouwe et al.’s closed testing procedure compares nested update methods and selects the most conservative one that significantly improves fit; it is a selection procedure, not a fixed sample-size rule. Steyerberg et al. evaluated validation samples from 200 patients/14 deaths through 10,000/700, but those are study design points, not recommendations that 14 events suffice for an update.

The familiar 10 events-per-parameter rule is a development heuristic, not an established rule for model updating; required sample size also depends on outcome prevalence, total sample, predictor count, and expected performance. Riley et al. give a development example requiring 23.1 events per parameter, illustrating why “10” is not a universal threshold. A 2026 framework extends sample-size planning to ML and updating: requirements depend on the chosen update strategy and performance target. Bayesian or shrinkage updating can regularize estimates toward the existing model, but does not establish a universal event minimum. **No validated events-per-adaptable-parameter rule for neural-network updating was found.**

## Measurement-indicator adaptation

**Not found:** in the targeted search, I found no verified clinical study that separately reweights individual measurement indicators or groups of tests across hospitals while holding the physiology/value component fixed. The closest antecedents already recorded in GAP are [Futoma et al. (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1097/CCE.0000000000000453) and [Zhou et al. (2023)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v206/zhou23b.html); neither demonstrates that per-feature or grouped testing-part adaptation ladder.

## References for a 2-page proposal

I would cite six references: **Lee et al. (2023)** for shift-dependent layer choice; **Kirichenko et al. (2023)** for head-only reweighting with a small target set; **Chen et al. (2023)** for direct cross-hospital sepsis transfer; **Guo et al. (2024)** for sample-efficient EHR adaptation; **Vergouwe et al. (2017)** for selecting among nested update levels; and **Riley et al. (2026)** for strategy-specific sample-size planning. Together they motivate a ladder whose flexibility is chosen with evidence about both shift and available labels, while leaving the project’s specific few-event observation-part adaptation as an open question.
