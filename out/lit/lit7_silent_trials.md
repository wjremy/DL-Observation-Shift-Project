- **Observed run lengths are precedents, not recommendations:** roughly 46 days to one year in the cited silent studies; Duke and Penn each report about three months.
- **Rates vary sharply by setting and definition:** 0.8% in the pediatric ward run, 3.3% in the Tufts hospital cohort, and 18.4% in the selected UCSD ED cohort. A pooled ICU cohort reported 18.8% Sepsis-3 incidence ([Moor et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eclinm.2023.102124)); that is a comparator, not a hospital-specific run-in rate.
- **Study-wide throughput, calculated from reported totals:** Tufts averages about 2,900 encounters and 95 cases/week over its 77-day period; the pediatric ward study averages about 166 encounters and 1.3 cases/week over a year. These are not per-hospital rates.
- For planning, multiply the **new hospital’s weekly eligible encounters** by its locally measured sepsis rate. Check whether the denominator is encounters, admissions, or unique patients, and whether “sepsis” uses the same definition.

**Local data for adaptation:** I found no study that estimates the data needed to fit a single parameter representing trust in testing patterns. The closest evidence concerns broader ICU model updating: Holder et al. report fine-tuning with about 1,500 target-cohort patients (about 30% of their local cohort) for predicting vasopressor use among severely septic patients ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1097/CCM.0000000000005175)); Moor et al. report improved external performance after fine-tuning on 10% of site data ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eclinm.2023.102124)). These are not sample-size targets for your parameter. The literature therefore does not support a defensible fixed case count for that update; it should be tied to the precision needed for the parameter estimate and the planned local validation.
