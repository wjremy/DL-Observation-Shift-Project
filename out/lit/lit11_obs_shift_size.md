## How much does observation shift matter between hospitals?

The direct evidence is mixed: measurement patterns can add predictive value across sites, but that value depends on the outcome, prediction timing, and similarity of care settings. Few studies isolate the effect of observation features in a genuinely held-out hospital comparison.

### Labels and testing

Sepsis-3 defines sepsis as infection-associated organ dysfunction, operationalized as an acute SOFA increase of at least 2 points. PhysioNet 2019 defined suspected infection from the earlier of IV-antibiotic and blood-culture timestamps: cultures had to follow antibiotics within 24 hours, or antibiotics follow cultures within 72 hours; antibiotics also had to continue for at least 72 hours. The label required a qualifying SOFA change within the specified window, and was set positive starting 6 hours before onset ([Singer et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2016.0287); [Reyna et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1097/CCM.0000000000004145)).

MIMIC/eICU Sepsis-3 benchmarks commonly combine suspected infection with SOFA, but implementations vary; Moor et al. used repeated antibiotic administrations as an alternative infection proxy in two databases. Schamoni et al. warn that using the same clinical criteria to define labels and predictors creates circularity. Here, SOFA-related lab values overlap with label construction, while testing masks can proxy the work-up. That is a plausible source of predictive signal, though the PhysioNet feature set does not itself include culture or antibiotic orders. ([Moor et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eclinm.2023.102124); [Schamoni et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.artmed.2019.101725))

### PhysioNet 2019 preprocessing

The challenge data came from different hospital systems and were already condensed into hourly bins; multiple measurements of a variable within an hour were summarized by their median. The organizers also merged LOINC codes for the same clinical parameter, such as serum and arterial hemoglobin. Missing and erroneous values were intentionally retained. These steps align the feature definitions and time grid, and hourly medians can blur within-hour testing order; they do not remove missingness or demonstrate that hospital testing patterns were harmonized. The challenge paper reported that most *value distributions* were similar between hospitals, which is not evidence that measurement rates were similar. ([Reyna et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1097/CCM.0000000000004145))

For its P19 experiments, Singh et al. dropped end-tidal CO₂ because it was available only at B, zero-imputed the remaining clinical features, and appended explicit binary observation masks. Thus the shared feature set was aligned while masks remained available to the model. ([Singh et al., 2021](https://medinform.jmir.org/2021/12/e25022))

### Takeaway

There is no stable “typical” size for observation shift. The clearest A→B sepsis ablation found a +.018 AUROC benefit for masks in a whole-record task, but a −.009 difference in the hourly task. Other studies find that observation features can help between similar settings and become less reliable across dissimilar ones; direct held-out-hospital ablations remain scarce. Sepsis is special because its operational label depends partly on clinical work-up and measured organ dysfunction. So testing patterns may predict both underlying illness and the process by which the benchmark label is generated.
